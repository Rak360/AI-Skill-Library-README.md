# Medical-Aesthetic Meta Ads Preflight Checklist

**Review date:** 2026-10-25  
**Purpose:** A practical publication gate for Facebook/Instagram advertising of aesthetic and medical services. This is an operational checklist, not legal advice or a guarantee that Meta will approve an ad. Platform policies and local law can change; re-open the official policy pages before each major campaign.

## 1. Policy and audience

- [ ] Open the current [Meta Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/) and review the applicable Health and Wellness, Personal Attributes, and other relevant rules.
- [ ] For cosmetic procedures/services subject to Meta's adult-only rule, set and verify the minimum audience age of 18+; do not target minors.
- [ ] Confirm that audience settings, exclusions, retargeting sources, and custom audiences comply with Meta policy and applicable privacy requirements.
- [ ] Do not build or upload audiences containing patient diagnoses, treatment histories, or other sensitive health data unless the use is explicitly lawful, authorized, and permitted by the platform. Prefer non-sensitive, consented first-party signals.

## 2. Copy and claims

- [ ] Describe the service neutrally; do not assert or imply that the viewer has a disease, skin condition, body defect, or other personal attribute.
- [ ] Avoid shaming or inferiority language about appearance, body parts, age, weight, skin, hair, or hygiene.
- [ ] Do not promise guaranteed, permanent, risk-free, or universally identical results.
- [ ] Do not invent treatment mechanisms, device capabilities, clinician credentials, session counts, prices, recovery times, or outcome percentages.
- [ ] Ensure every clinical or comparative claim is supported by current evidence and approved by the responsible clinician.
- [ ] Explain material limitations or variability when needed; do not use a disclaimer to try to rescue a misleading headline.

**Safer copy direction:** “Explore clinician-led options for uneven skin tone”  
**Avoid:** “Do you have melasma? Fix your damaged skin now.”

## 3. Images and video

- [ ] Use authentic, authorized media; retain documented consent for identifiable patients.
- [ ] Do not fabricate, materially retouch, or misrepresent clinical before/after results.
- [ ] If showing before/after content, verify the current Meta rule for the service and adult audience, use comparable conditions where practical, and avoid implying that an individual result is guaranteed.
- [ ] Check that generated or stock visuals are not presented as actual patients, actual treatment results, or real clinic equipment.
- [ ] Manually inspect on-image text, logos, flags, uniforms, devices, skin details, and subtitles before publication.

## 4. Landing page, forms, and tracking

- [ ] The landing page describes the same service, price, offer, limitations, and provider as the ad.
- [ ] Include accurate contact details and a clear privacy notice.
- [ ] Request only information necessary to handle the enquiry. Do not collect detailed medical history in a general ad lead form unless an approved, secure process and lawful basis are in place.
- [ ] Check Pixel/CAPI and analytics events for accidental transmission of patient names, health conditions, free-text form answers, or other sensitive data.
- [ ] Verify that consent and data-retention settings match the actual collection and use of information.

## 5. Final approval gate

A campaign should be marked **PASS** only after all checks are complete:

1. **Marketing owner:** copy, offer, audience, destination, tracking.
2. **Clinical reviewer:** medical accuracy, evidence, limitations, consent.
3. **Policy/privacy reviewer:** platform rules, audience age, data handling, local requirements.

If any claim, consent, age setting, privacy control, or policy interpretation is unclear, mark **HOLD — REVIEW REQUIRED** and do not publish. Keep a dated copy of the approved creative and landing page for audit purposes.
